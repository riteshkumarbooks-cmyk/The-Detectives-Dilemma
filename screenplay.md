
THE DETECTIVE'S DILEMMA

Written as an Original Television Screenplay

FADE IN:

---

EXT. CITY SKYLINE — NIGHT

Rain falls in silver curtains over a city that has given up pretending to be beautiful. Neon signs bleed into the wet pavement — red, gold, the cold blue of a pharmacy cross. A gargoyle watches from a cornice. Below it, a cat watches the gargoyle.

The cat is not impressed.

SUPER: "Episode 1 — The First Audience"

---

INT. LLOYD'S OFFICE — CONTINUOUS

A room that smells of old ambition. Water stain on the ceiling in the shape of a question mark — or a noose, depending on your mood. A half-empty bottle of rye on the desk. On the floor, case files arranged with the chaotic logic of a man who tells himself it's a system.

LLOYD SCHWARZER (30s) sits behind the desk in a rumpled suit that was once expensive. Handsome in the way a ruined church is handsome — the structure is all still there, but something burned. He tosses a tennis ball at the wall. Catches it. Tosses again. He has been doing this for approximately forty-five minutes.

A TABBY CAT — LUNA — sits on the windowsill, watching the rain with the philosophical detachment of a retired cardinal.

LUNA (V.O.)
(dryly)
Day sixteen with no client. He finished the rye on Tuesday. He started talking to the wall on Wednesday. The wall hasn't answered. I consider this a mercy.

Lloyd catches the ball and doesn't throw it again. He tilts his head.

LLOYD
You smell that?

Luna lifts her nose. Her nostrils flare precisely once.

LUNA (V.O.)
Jasmine. White musk. Something French.
Something afraid.

A knock at the frosted glass door.

LLOYD
It's open.

The door swings and LADY MARGAUX DE VALOIS enters the room like weather. She is 30s, French, with cheekbones that could file taxes and eyes that have seen things they elected not to discuss. She wears a black silk coat that costs more than Lloyd's rent. She clutches a leather folder to her chest like a shield.

She looks at the office. She looks at Lloyd. She looks at the cat.

MARGAUX
Mr. Schwarzer.

LLOYD
Lady Margaux.

He doesn't stand immediately. Then he does. Manners arriving fashionably late.

LLOYD (CONT'D)
Sit down. Ignore the files. They're decorative.

Margaux sits, spine perfectly erect, and places the folder on the desk.

MARGAUX
I have received letters.

She opens the folder. Lloyd picks up the first. Typed, no signature. The paper itself is expensive — cotton rag, cream-colored.

INSERT — THE LETTER: "Your beauty is a borrowed thing. One day the lender will come to collect."

Lloyd's jaw tightens by one millimeter. He sets it down and picks up the next.

LLOYD
How many?

MARGAUX
Seven. Over six weeks. And three days ago —

She pauses. Her fingers press together in her lap.

MARGAUX (CONT'D)
The chandelier in the east foyer fell. It weighs four hundred pounds. I had been standing beneath it thirty seconds earlier.

LLOYD
Police report?

MARGAUX
Captain Crawford was very thorough. Very dismissive.

Lloyd leans back. Studies her face the way a jeweler studies a stone — looking for the flaw that tells you whether it's real.

LLOYD
What aren't you telling me, Lady Margaux?

A silence drops into the room. Luna watches it fall.

MARGAUX
(quietly)
Everything. For now.

LLOYD
My rate is two thousand a day.

MARGAUX
I'll pay six.

Lloyd picks up the tennis ball. Sets it down again.

LLOYD
When do I start?

MARGAUX
(standing)
You already have.

She leaves. The jasmine lingers. Lloyd stares at the empty doorway.

LUNA (V.O.)
She's lying. Or holding back. The distinction will matter more than you think.

INT. PRECINCT — DETECTIVE BULLPEN — DAY

Fluorescent lights that make everyone look like they have six months to live. DETECTIVE ASHLEY MARTINEZ (30s) stands at a whiteboard covered in photographs — three women, all brunette, all beautiful, all gone. She wears a leather jacket like it's armor and like she earned it.

She's the most competent person in any room she enters. She knows this and finds it mildly exhausting.

Lloyd appears at the door.

ASHLEY
I don't have time.

LLOYD
Good morning to you too.

ASHLEY
Third victim this month. Brunette, jasmine perfume, strangled with a double fisherman's knot. He takes the jewelry afterward. Specifically rubies.

She taps the board.

ASHLEY (CONT'D)
Someone hired you today.

LLOYD
Occupation hazard of being perceptive.

ASHLEY
Someone French?

LLOYD
You're doing the thing where you show off.

ASHLEY
(turning to face him)
You have jasmine on your collar, Lloyd.

He looks down. He does.

LLOYD
She received death threats. Chandelier fell. She fits the victim profile almost exactly.

ASHLEY
Almost.

LLOYD
The victims are approximations. Copies of something. Someone.

Their eyes meet. A current runs between them — old electricity, familiar and dangerous.

ASHLEY
Don't get involved in my case.

LLOYD
I'm not getting involved in your case.

ASHLEY
You're already involved in my case.

A beat. Then her radio crackles — jewelry store robbery, downtown, suspect fleeing on foot. She's already moving.

LLOYD
I'll just walk with you.

They run.

---

EXT. CITY ALLEY — CONTINUOUS

A thief in a grey hoodie sprints through the maze of fire escapes and overflowing dumpsters. Lloyd and Ashley are fifty yards back, closing. The thief hits a chain-link fence, scales it with liquid ease.

Ashley draws her weapon.

ASHLEY
POLICE! STOP!

The thief reaches the fire escape and climbs. A bag tears. RUBIES scatter down like broken rain, bouncing off metal stairs, ringing against the wet pavement.

They both freeze and watch the thief vanish into the roofline.

LLOYD
(looking at the rubies)
He only takes them from corpses. He doesn't steal them any other way.

Ashley holsters her weapon. Looks at him.

ASHLEY
How do you know that?

LLOYD
Because you'd have linked the robbery to the murders already if he did. You're good, Ashley. He's escalating.

The moment almost becomes something else. Almost.

CAPTAIN CRAWFORD (50s) arrives like a weather event — barrel-chested, thunderous, the face of a man who has eaten nothing but disappointment for twenty years. He surveys the scene.

CRAWFORD
Get that civilian OUT of my crime scene.

ASHLEY
(quietly, to Lloyd)
Go. And be careful with your French widow.

Lloyd goes. He walks past the rubies without picking one up.

---

EXT. ART GALLERY DISTRICT — LATER THAT NIGHT

Lloyd walks home past galleries that glow like aquariums. In the window of one, enormous canvases — all faces, blurred, yearning, beautiful and wrong.

A man stands at the window, studying his own work.

LUKE VANCE (30s). He is beautiful in the way that certain dangerous things are beautiful — a still lake in November, a lit fuse. Dark eyes. Paint under his nails. He dresses like he doesn't own a mirror but could model for one.

He speaks without turning around.

LUKE
You've been standing there for thirty seconds.

LLOYD
You paint what people don't want to see.

LUKE
(finally turning)
I paint what they can't stop looking at. There's a difference.

Lloyd studies the canvases. Every face is a woman's. Every woman is a variation on the same woman. He files this away.

LUKE (CONT'D)
You're investigating the widow.

LLOYD
I'm taking a walk.

LUKE
(softly)
I saw her. Near the clock tower on Tuesday. A figure was following her. Tall. In a dark coat. She didn't see him.

LLOYD
Why didn't you call the police?

Luke smiles. It doesn't reach his eyes — not because he's hiding something, but because his eyes live somewhere else entirely.

LUKE
Police don't understand nuance.

Lloyd's phone rings. He answers.

MARGAUX (OVER PHONE)
(whispering)
He's here, Lloyd. He found me.

Then nothing. Line dead.

Lloyd is already running.

INT. CHATEAU MARGAUX — ENTRANCE — NIGHT

SUPER: "Episode 2 — Household Secrets"

BORIS (50s) fills the doorway with the authority of a load-bearing wall. He is built like a Soviet tank and has the face of a man who has survived things polite society doesn't discuss. He looks at Lloyd with professional skepticism.

BORIS
Madame is not available.

LLOYD
She called me. Move.

Boris doesn't move. Lloyd moves him, mostly by walking through the space Boris occupies with sufficient conviction.

---

INT. CHATEAU MARGAUX — SOLARIUM — CONTINUOUS

THIERRY DE VALOIS (21) and CELESTE DE VALOIS (22) are in the middle of an argument that has clearly been running for years. Thierry is sweating through a good shirt. He has the look of a man who owes money to people with short patience and long memories. Celeste wears combat boots under an evening dress, arms crossed, furious at the specific gravity of the room.

They both stop when Lloyd enters.

CELESTE
(to Lloyd, studying him)
You must be the detective.

She says "detective" the way some people say "interesting mistake."

Margaux descends the stairs, composed now, the fear filed neatly behind her eyes.

MARGAUX
I saw a shadow at my bedroom door. It tried the handle.

THIERRY
It was probably the wind —

CELESTE
It's never the wind, Thierry. Wind doesn't try door handles.

ANABELLE (28) arrives with chamomile tea on a silver tray. She is slender, dark-haired, with the invisible quality of someone who has perfected the art of occupying space without claiming it. A botany degree and a chemistry certificate hang in her small room down the hall. She has never mentioned them.

She sets the cup before Lloyd with a warm, attentive smile.

Luna, who has been riding in Lloyd's coat pocket, puts her nose over the rim and recoils.

LUNA (V.O.)
Bitter almonds.

Lloyd's hand moves. The cup goes sideways. Tea splashes across the fern in the corner.

Everyone watches the fern.

The leaves shrivel. The stem goes black.

ANABELLE
(alarmed, apparently)
Oh, Madame, I'm so sorry — I must have grabbed the wrong —

LLOYD
(quietly)
Don't apologize. Leave.

Anabelle leaves. Her face as she goes: unreadable. Not guilty, not innocent. Simply blank.

---

INT. CHATEAU MARGAUX — GARDEN SHED — LATER

Luna leads Lloyd through the dark garden, following a trail of fine white dust on the flagstones. The shed door is unlocked — it shouldn't be.

Inside: surveillance monitors. Multiple screens. Live feeds of Margaux's bedroom, bathroom, library. The feeds are crisp, recent, professional.

On the wall: photographs of Margaux. Dozens. Her eyes scratched out with something red. Not pen. Something else.

Turpentine. Paint.

A door at the back is still swinging.

---

EXT. CHATEAU MARGAUX — GROUNDS — CONTINUOUS

Lloyd runs. The FIGURE IN A DARK HOODIE breaks from the tree line, sprints across the lawn. Lloyd loses the figure at the garden wall. A grey sedan fishtails out of the service drive.

He catches the plate, partial: 7-G-K.

On the ground: a clove cigarette, barely smoked. He picks it up in a handkerchief.

Luna pads up beside him and sniffs it deliberately.

LUNA (V.O.)
Turpentine. Cloves. The smell of the gallery window.

EXT. CITY — ATMOSPHERE SHOT — NIGHT

A neon sign over a jazz club stutters: B-LUE. BLUE. B-LUE. Rain runs between the letters. A clock tower reads 2:17 a.m. The hands don't move.

---

INT. LUKE'S STUDIO — NIGHT

SUPER: "Episode 3 — Blood and Jasmine"

The studio assaults the eye. Every surface — walls, floor, ceiling struts — covered in paintings. All Margaux. Her face in oils, acrylics, charcoal, watercolor. The same face rendered a thousand ways by the same obsessive hand.

Lloyd stands in the center of this shrine and makes himself look at all of it.

Luke opens the door in his bathrobe, unsurprised.

LUKE
She's beautiful.

LLOYD
(carefully)
She is.

LUKE
You don't understand. When you look at something truly beautiful, there's a violence in it. It demands something from you.

His eyes drift to a small corner of the room. A charcoal sketch, small, framed in cheap wood: two CHILDREN in dirty clothes, holding hands before a burning house. Their faces are turned away.

Luke sees Lloyd looking. He crosses the room in two steps and covers it with a cloth.

LUKE (CONT'D)
(quietly)
Just a nightmare.

Lloyd notes the size of the painting. The size of the two children. The fact that one is a girl.

---

EXT./INT. CHARITY GALA — ESTABLISHING AND CONTINUOUS

Crystal and candlelight. Society in its natural habitat — competitive, anxious, beautiful, and broke. Margaux enters in emerald silk. Lloyd beside her, cleaned up enough to pass.

In the garage beneath the venue, before they enter:

CELESTE sits on the hood of the Rolls Royce, sharpening a pencil with a box cutter. She looks up at Lloyd. The blade catches light.

CELESTE
Going to protect her all night?

LLOYD
That's the job.

CELESTE
(running her thumb along the dull edge)
She doesn't deserve protecting. She collects people and puts them on display. Like a museum.

Lloyd looks at her. The fury in her face is real. So is something else — something she hasn't named yet.

LLOYD
Are you coming inside?

CELESTE
(small, cold smile)
Not my scene.

She doesn't come inside. She stays in the garage.

During the afternoon — hours before the gala, while Boris is occupied upstairs with pre-event duties — Anabelle is in this same garage. She has a small leather kit. A single razor blade. She knows exactly where to cut.

INT. GALA — CONTINUOUS

THIERRY is at the bar, alone, working through his second whiskey. His collar is too tight. His eyes keep moving.

THIERRY
(to Lloyd, low)
They're taking bets. On when she snaps. On when the "cursed widow" finally eats herself.

He finishes the drink.

THIERRY (CONT'D)
She doesn't deserve it. She's terrified. I'm asking you — just keep her safe tonight.

Across the room, Lloyd clocks LUKE at a table, sketching Margaux on a cocktail napkin. His face: rapturous, private. The rest of the room doesn't exist for him.

---

EXT. GALA — FRONT APPROACH — LATER

The Rolls Royce rolls up for pickup. Boris behind the wheel. Then: the car begins to drift. Boris's arms are braced on the wheel, fighting it. He pumps the brakes — nothing. The car accelerates.

It jumps the curb. Crosses the sidewalk.

LLOYD
(grabbing Margaux)
DOWN —

He takes her to the ground. The Rolls Royce obliterates the ornamental fountain in an explosion of marble and water.

Silence. Then car alarms.

Boris climbs from the wreckage. Shaking.

Lloyd inspects the brake line. A single cut — surgical, clean. One razor slash. Not panic, not vandalism. Execution.

He looks up at the building. Luke was inside at his table all evening. Celeste was in the garage but had no reason and no knowledge of brake lines. Boris was driving — no motive to kill himself.

Someone else was in that garage. Someone with a chemistry background. Someone with a razor kit. Someone with full estate access and no one watching her.

He tucks the knowledge away. Luna watches from a bench, tail still, eyes moving.

INT. COFFEE SHOP — DAY

SUPER: "Episode 4 — Mirrors and Smoke"

Lloyd and Luke across a small table. Two coffees. Luke has paint on his jaw.

LUKE
Tuesday night I was at the estate — painting the tree line. I saw a tall figure in a hood at the gate. Standing very still. Watching.

LLOYD
You watched someone watch her and you didn't call anyone.

LUKE
(simply)
No.

LLOYD
Why?

LUKE
Because I wanted to see what he wanted. I wanted to understand him.

He says this with complete serenity. Lloyd studies him.

LLOYD
Do you understand him?

Luke's eyes shift — just slightly. Something moves behind them. A different person, very briefly, before the curtain drops again.

LUKE
(softly)
Better than you'd think.

---

INT. BOUTIQUE — DRESSING ROOM CORRIDOR — AFTERNOON

Ashley finds Lloyd by the jewelry counter. Margaux and BECKY (40s) — big laugh, bigger handbag, the last gasp of a social life funded by credit — are disappearing into dressing rooms.

Becky is aggressively friendly in the way of someone who needs the room to like her.

ASHLEY
(low, to Lloyd)
The killer leaves a specific pendant on each victim. Places it after death. It's a signature — he's staging them.

LLOYD
Staging them as what?

ASHLEY
We're working on it.

Then: smoke. From the changing room corridor.

The fire is fast and purposeful — oily rags, accelerant fed through a ventilation intake. Becky's changing room fills with black smoke and heat. The mirror explodes outward.

Lloyd wades in and drags Becky out, hacking, mascara streaming, a pink dress half-on.

---

EXT. BOUTIQUE — CONTINUOUS

Ashley is already reading the intake.

ASHLEY
Molotov, basically. Through the vent. Whoever did this thought Margaux would be in that room.

She looks at Lloyd.

ASHLEY (CONT'D)
Someone tried to kill her.

LLOYD
Or scare her. Or send a message.

ASHLEY
Come in for the debrief.

Margaux takes Lloyd's arm, pale, trembling. Lloyd looks between them — the woman who needs him and the woman who knows what she's doing.

ASHLEY (CONT'D)
(reading his face)
Of course.

She walks away. Her back says everything her voice did not.

INT. CHATEAU MARGAUX — MARGAUX'S BEDROOM — NIGHT

Candlelight. Margaux tends a burn on Lloyd's arm with careful hands. This is how intimacy begins between careful people — through small administered kindnesses.

MARGAUX
Why do you do this? The work.

LLOYD
Someone has to.

MARGAUX
That isn't an answer.

LLOYD
No. It's not.

She looks at him. He looks back. Something falls away from both of them, briefly.

EXT. CHATEAU MARGAUX — GARDEN — LATER

Lloyd stands on the balcony. Below: THIERRY at the incinerator. He's feeding something into it — a hoodie, gloves. He works with the hurried guilt of a man who almost did something terrible and is erasing the proof of how close he got.

Luna sits on the balcony rail.

LUNA (V.O.)
Lighter fluid. He was near the boutique that night. He went there. But the fire was already burning when he arrived.

Lloyd watches Thierry until the fire dies.

INT. CHATEAU MARGAUX — LIBRARY — DAY

SUPER: "Episode 5 — Family Blood"

Thierry breaks in five minutes. He was never built for carrying secrets.

THIERRY
Someone slipped a note under my door. Cash for the supplies. Said it was a prank — smoke bomb through the vent. Ruin the opening, claim the smoke damage. No one gets hurt, easy money.

LLOYD
But you didn't do it.

THIERRY
(shaking his head)
I went there. I had the device in my bag. I was standing at the alley entrance and I — I couldn't. I went home. I swear I never touched the vent. But when I heard the building was on fire I panicked. I burned the clothes to cut the connection.

LLOYD
Someone knew you had the supplies. Knew you'd be nearby. Knew you'd run.

Lloyd stares at him.

LLOYD (CONT'D)
The note, the cash, the setup — it wasn't for you to do the job, Thierry. It was for you to be found near it when someone else did.

Thierry looks up. The blood leaves his face.

THIERRY
(barely audible)
I was the alibi. For whoever actually lit it.

EXT. ESTATE STABLES — AFTERNOON

The horses are restless. Luna pads along the fence line, nose working.

BECKY feeds the stallion — Midnight, sixteen hands of nervous energy. She coos and offers an apple.

Luna stops at the feed bucket.

LUNA (V.O.)
Datura. Devil's Breath. Enough to send a horse mad.

Midnight rears. Margaux, who has come up behind Becky, is thrown ten feet — a crack of impact, a scream that dies into silence.

ANABELLE runs toward the horse, reaching for the bridle. The horse kicks. The sound is terrible. Anabelle goes down — ribs, internal — and doesn't move.

In the chaos of the ambulance, Lloyd holds the feed bucket and stares at Becky. She goes white.

BECKY
I didn't — Lloyd, I didn't put anything in the —

LLOYD
You touched it. You were the only one here.

BECKY
(running)
I have to —

He can't chase her. The paramedics are loading Anabelle. She is terribly still, and her face — as the oxygen mask goes on — looks almost peaceful.

LUNA (V.O.)
She looks peaceful because she planned for exactly this. The horse was supposed to kill Margaux. The kick was supposed to happen to Margaux, not to Anabelle. But Anabelle stepped into the path of the horse herself — a calculated sacrifice. Injured trying to help, she becomes above suspicion. A maid who nearly died protecting her mistress.

Lloyd doesn't know this yet. He watches the ambulance go.

INT. ASHLEY'S APARTMENT — NIGHT

Lloyd arrives with dirt on his face and someone else's blood on his shirt. Ashley opens the door and lets him in without a word, because she knows all his entrances and what they cost him.

Luna sits between them on the couch and looks at both of them with the deliberate patience of a professional.

LUNA (V.O.)
You are juggling knives, Lloyd. And you've invited audiences.

ASHLEY
Sit down.

LLOYD
I'm fine.

ASHLEY
You're bleeding through your shirt. Sit down.

He sits down. She finds gauze. This is also intimacy — the functional, grounded kind. The kind that endures.

Lloyd is in the surveillance van forty feet up the block. Luna sits on the equipment panel.

LUKE steps from an alley, sketchbook in hand. He stops when he sees Ashley. His face transforms — the way a tuning fork transforms when it finds its note.

LUKE

Margaux? You look... perfect. Better than the others.

LUKE (CONT'D)
A tragedy in green.

LLOYD

LLOYD (CONT'D)
Someone else was in this street. Someone who wanted to kill Margaux while Luke took the blame. Someone who knew about the sting tonight.

BECKY
